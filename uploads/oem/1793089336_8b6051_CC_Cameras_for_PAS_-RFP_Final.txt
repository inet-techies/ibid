3. Introduction to the Project
3.1 Background
The Government of India's Swachh Bharat Mission (Urban) 2.0 envisages all cities becoming "Garbage Free", with sustained Open Defecation Free (ODF++) status and comprehensive source segregation, scientific processing and remediation of legacy dumpsites. In terms of the Operational Guidelines issued by the Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs, Government of India, the Information, Education and Communication (IEC) Component of the Mission specifically provides for citizen behaviour-change interventions, including technology-enabled surveillance and real-time public messaging at chronically vulnerable locations, in furtherance of the Mission's Garbage Free City (GFC) star-rating protocol.
The Greater Hyderabad Municipal Corporation, constituted under the Greater Hyderabad Municipal Corporation Act, 1955, has, after due field verification by its Sanitation and Solid Waste Management Wing, identified Garbage Vulnerable Points (GVPs), Sweeping Points and Hotspots across its jurisdiction for technology-driven surveillance, real-time citizen deterrence and eventual elimination. This initiative is taken forward in continuation of the Corporation's ongoing Swachh Hyderabad drive, comprising barricading, wall art and GVP-to-park conversion undertaken at field level by Circle Sanitation staff.
This RFP is issued for the selection of a competent System Integrator or Implementation Agency for the procurement, design, supply, installation, testing, commissioning, integration and comprehensive Operation and Maintenance of CCTV cameras and IoT-based Public Address and citizen-command systems at 2,500 locations within GHMC limits, strictly on a turnkey basis.
3.2 Objectives
Continuous visual monitoring and video-based evidence capture at all contracted locations, to deter unauthorised dumping of garbage.
Real-time, sensor-based detection of dumping events at each location using IoT devices, independent of manual control-room intervention.
Instant on-site audio and visual command at the moment garbage is dumped, discouraging the act and directing citizens to designated collection points and door-to-door collection timings.
Automated alerts, snapshots and video clips to the Integrated Command and Control Centre for monitoring, evidence-based enforcement and issue of penalty or challan under the applicable bye-laws.
Escalation of garbage accumulation beyond a configurable threshold to the concerned Sanitation Inspector, field staff and Deputy Executive Engineer (SWM), so that clearance can be initiated promptly and field responsiveness can be measured.
A centralised dashboard providing real-time and historical analytics of all contracted locations, hotspot-closure tracking and trend reporting to support data-driven policy decisions.
Measurable, sustained elimination of GVPs, Sweeping Points and Hotspots, supporting the Corporation's progress toward higher Garbage Free City star ratings and its Swachh Survekshan indicators.

3.4 Contracted Quantity and Substitution of Locations
This clause is central to the commercial structure of this Project and Bidders shall read it with particular care. It fixes the contracted quantity while conferring on the Authority an unqualified right to change the locations at which that quantity is deployed.
The contracted quantity for this Project is, and shall throughout the contract period remain, two thousand five hundred (2,500) locations, which may increase or decrease by reason of any substitution, relocation, addition or deletion of individual locations made under this clause.
The Authority may at any time during the contract period, at its sole discretion and without assigning any reason, substitute any location or locations with other locations within GHMC limits. The Authority may equally add a location to, or delete a location from, the deployment list, provided that the total number of locations at which the system is deployed shall at all times be brought back to, and shall not exceed, two thousand five hundred.
The right at sub-clause (2) may be exercised on any ground, including successful elimination or closure of a GVP consequent to behavioural change, conversion of a GVP into a park or other public amenity, road widening or other civil works, change in dumping patterns, identification of a new vulnerable point, adverse site conditions, or repeated theft or vandalism at a location.
Elimination of a GVP is an objective of this Project, and the Bidder shall not be entitled to treat the closure of a location as a reduction in the contracted quantity. Where a location is closed, the Authority shall notify a substitute location and the Bidder shall relocate the installation to it. The Bidder shall not derive any benefit from the continuance of a GVP, nor suffer any loss from its elimination.
A substitution shall be effected by written notice from the Additional Commissioner (Sanitation), identifying the location to be vacated by its Collection Point Code and the substitute location by its Collection Point Code or, where the substitute is not on the master list, by its name, Circle, Zone and geo-coordinates. The Bidder shall complete the relocation within thirty (30) days of such notice.
Relocation comprises safe de-installation of all equipment at the vacated location, restoration of the vacated site to its original condition to the satisfaction of the DEE (SWM) of concerned circle, transportation, and re-installation, testing and commissioning at the substitute location, together with reconfiguration in the dashboard, the asset register and the Integrated Command and Control Centre so that the substitute location is monitored without interruption.
Relocation shall be paid for at the firm relocation rate per location quoted at Item 15 of the Price Schedule. That rate shall be firm for the contract period and shall be the only sum payable on account of a substitution. No mobilisation charge, demobilisation charge, one-time charge, idle charge, escalation or other sum whatsoever shall be payable on account of any substitution.
The Bidder shall carry out, at the relocation rate and without demur, up to two hundred and fifty (250) relocations in each year of the contract period, being ten (10) per cent of the contracted quantity. Where the Authority requires relocations in excess of that number in any year, the excess shall be carried out at the same firm rate, and the Bidder shall not decline or delay such relocation, but the Bidder may seek an extension of the completion period at sub-clause (5) in respect of the excess.
Where equipment is lost, damaged or rendered unserviceable in the course of a relocation, the replacement shall be at the cost of the Bidder. Where equipment is stolen or vandalised at a location, Clause 11.4 shall apply.
No claim shall lie against the Authority for loss of profit, loss of opportunity, or any consequential loss whatsoever by reason of the exercise of the rights conferred by this clause. Bidders shall price their bids on the footing that the identity of the 2,500 locations will change during the contract period, and that the number will not.
The equipment configuration at an individual location may vary with site geometry, the number of approach roads and field conditions established during the joint site survey. Such variation shall be dealt with under Clause 4.2 and shall be paid for at the unit rates in the Price Schedule. It shall not alter the contracted quantity of locations.
Illustration. If, in the second year of the contract period, forty locations are eliminated following sustained behavioural change and the Authority notifies forty substitute locations, the Bidder shall relocate the installations within thirty days of notice and shall be paid forty times the relocation rate at Item 15. 4. Scope of Work
4.1 Overview
The Scope of Work shall be executed by the Bidder on a turnkey, single-point-responsibility basis, and comprises:
Joint site survey, validation of locations and site-readiness assessment.
Supply, installation, testing and commissioning of CCTV surveillance cameras at each contracted location.
Supply, installation, testing and commissioning of IoT-based garbage-dump-detection sensors and Public Address and citizen-command units at each contracted location.
Supply and erection of mounting infrastructure, including Smart Poles where required, together with power, solar and connectivity infrastructure at each contracted location.
Supply, installation and commissioning of the central recording, storage, networking and display infrastructure at the notified Command Centres.
Development, hosting and commissioning of the centralised Video Management Systems, dashboard and analytics platform, and its integration with the systems of the Corporation.
Relocation of installations between locations as directed under Clause 3.4.
Comprehensive Operation and Maintenance, including manned shift-wise monitoring at the Integrated Command and Control Centre, for the contract period.
Training and capacity building of Corporation personnel and control-room operators.
Provision of mobile based display for officers for monitoring.
4.2 Site Survey and Validation
The Bidder shall conduct a joint physical survey of each contracted location together with the Deputy Executive Engineer (SWM) of the respective Circle, to finalise the site-wise hardware requirement, being the number and type of cameras, the IoT and Public Address units, the mounting structure, the power source and the networking equipment.
The survey shall assess line of sight for the camera, sensor placement feasibility, availability of a power source and of an existing pole or civil structure, network connectivity by fibre or 4G or 5G, ambient lighting, exposure to tampering, and any civil work required.
The Bidder shall submit a Site Feasibility Report and a Bill of Materials for each location, identifying the location by its Collection Point Code and recording its geo-coordinates, duly countersigned by the concerned Deputy Executive Engineer (SWM), for approval of the Competent Authority before execution at that location.
Each location shall be covered by not fewer than one fixed IP camera. Where site geometry, the number of approach roads or the extent of the dumping area so requires, the Competent Authority may approve an additional fixed camera or a PTZ camera at that location, and the additional unit shall be paid for at the unit rates in the Price Schedule.
The survey of the locations identified at Clause 3.3 shall be completed within the period stated at Clause 8.2. The survey of a location notified subsequently under Clause 3.4 shall be completed within fifteen (15) days of the notice.
4.3 CCTV Camera System
4.3.1 Deployment
Cameras shall be mounted on the Smart Pole, on an existing pole approved by the Authority, on a wall, or on a purpose-built structure, at a height sufficient to avoid tampering/Pilferage while retaining clear coverage of the GVP.
The mounting position shall be such that the camera field of view covers the whole of the identified GVP area and, so far as practicable, the approach to it.
Cameras shall be positioned so as not to survey the interior of any private premises, and the Bidder shall apply privacy masking to any part of the field of view that unavoidably includes private premises.
4.3.2 Functional and technical requirements
Fixed IP bullet or box camera of not less than 4 MP resolution (Provision to identification of facial features and vehicle number clearly) , with an integrated 4G/5G module and an amplified audio output capable of driving the Public Address speaker specified at Clause 4.4.
Continuous recording on a twenty-four hour, seven day basis, with local edge storage at the location and retrieval from the central Network Video Recorder and Video Management System.
Day and night recording capability, being colour-in-low-light or infrared, suitable for outdoor, unlit and poorly lit locations.
Weatherproof and vandal-resistant housing, rated not less than IP66 and IK10 or equivalent, suited to outdoor deployment in Hyderabad conditions.
Embedded or server-side video analytics capable of object and garbage-drop detection, loitering and dumping-activity detection, and generation of an event trigger capable of being linked to the IoT announcement unit and to the control-room alert.
Tamper detection, comprising camera blocking, defocus, view obstruction and physical movement, with automatic alert generation.
Remote configuration, health monitoring and firmware update capability from the Integrated Command and Control Centre.
Certification as BIS, CE or FCC compliant as applicable, and compliance with the technical guidelines of the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology for surveillance and IoT device security.

4.4 IoT-based Public Address and Citizen Command System
4.4.1 Purpose
Each contracted location shall be equipped with an IoT-based detection and Public Address unit that identifies the act of garbage being dumped in real time and immediately issues an audible and visual command or deterrent message to the citizen at the location, functioning as an instant, automated, on-site nudge and enforcement mechanism independent of manual control-room intervention.
4.4.2 Detection mechanism
Passive infrared motion sensors, ultrasonic or infrared proximity sensors, or weight and vibration sensors, as appropriate to site conditions, to detect the physical act of an object being deposited within the monitored zone.
The detection logic shall, wherever feasible, be corroborated with the video analytics event trigger, so as to minimise false positives and to distinguish a pedestrian passing the location from an actual act of dumping.
Configurable sensitivity and configurable detection-zone boundaries, to suit the geometry of each location.
The false-positive rate shall not exceed the threshold at Clause 11.3.4, and the Bidder shall tune the detection logic at any location where that threshold is exceeded.
4.4.3 Public Address and citizen response unit
On confirmed detection, the unit shall trigger an instant pre-recorded audio announcement through a weatherproof outdoor speaker, in Telugu and Urdu, with Hindi and English as secondary languages, in line with the multilingual citizenry of the Corporation.
The announcement shall convey a directive not to dump garbage at the location, together with information on 4 way segregation, Swachh Auto Tipper collection timings and any other solid waste management and sanitation related messages applicable to that location.
An accompanying visual deterrent, being a flashing LED, warning light or digital signage board displaying a text message, shall be triggered simultaneously where specified by the Authority.
The message content and library shall be configurable and updatable remotely by the Authority. Multiple message variants shall be supported so as to avoid habituation, and the system shall cycle between them.
Announcement volume shall be configurable per location and per time of day, and shall be capable of being restricted during night hours so as to comply with the Noise Pollution (Regulation and Control) Rules, 2000. The Bidder shall configure a reduced night-time volume, or suppress the announcement, at any location falling within a silence zone as defined under those Rules, as directed by the Authority.
Each detection event shall automatically generate a timestamped alert with a snapshot and short video clip, sent in real time to the dashboard at the Integrated Command and Control Centre and, where integrated, to the enforcement application of the Corporation for follow-up action.
4.5 Garbage Accumulation and Volume Escalation
In addition to the citizen-facing announcement, the system shall function as an escalation tool for the sanitation field machinery of the Corporation.
Where the sensors or the video analytics detect that garbage has accumulated at a location beyond a configurable threshold, whether by reason of repeated dumping events without intervening clearance or by visual estimation of pile size exceeding the set limit, the system shall automatically generate a distinct escalation alert, over and above the citizen announcement.
Such escalation alert shall be sent in real time, with the Collection Point Code, location name, timestamp and snapshot or clip, to the concerned Sanitation Inspector, field staff and Deputy Executive Engineer (SWM) of the Circle, through SMS, the dashboard, and the citizen mobile application of the Corporation or a dedicated mobile notification, as integrated.
The threshold for triggering escalation, and the list of officers to be notified, shall be configurable by the Authority at Circle and Zone level and updatable from the dashboard without recourse to the Bidder.
The system shall record the time of the escalation alert and the time at which clearance is marked complete, shall compute the time taken for clearance, and shall report clearance performance by location, Circle and Zone, so as to enable the Corporation to monitor field responsiveness.
Escalation alerts and clearance times shall be retained in accordance with Clause 4.3.3 and shall be available for audit.
4.6 Networking, Central Monitoring and Integration
4.6.1 Central infrastructure
The Bidder shall supply, install and commission at the Integrated Command and Control Centre the servers for live view, the Network Video Recorders, the display monitors for pop-up event view, the racks, the Layer 3 switching, and all allied infrastructure specified in the Price Schedule.
The Network Video Recorder capacity shall be sized to the retention requirement at Clause 4.3.3 across the full contracted quantity, with not less than fifteen per cent spare channel capacity.
The Bidder shall provide and maintain the central bandwidth specified at the Price Schedule for the contract period, and shall be responsible for its adequacy to carry the aggregate feed of all contracted locations at the configured stream settings.
4.6.2 Dashboard
The dashboard shall provide:
Live view and playback of all cameras, with role-based access.
A GIS-based map view of all contracted locations, Zone-wise and Circle-wise, with real-time status shown as active, inactive or in alert.
An event log of all detected dumping incidents with time, Collection Point Code, location, snapshot and clip.
Analytics and management information reports, including Ward-wise, Circle-wise and Zone-wise trends, time-of-day analysis, repeat-offender hotspot analysis, clearance performance under Clause 4.5, and hotspot-closure tracking.
A relocation register recording every substitution effected under Clause 3.4, with the date of notice, the vacated and substitute Collection Point Codes, and the date of recommissioning.
Role-based user access control for officials of the Sanitation and Solid Waste Management Wing, at Ward, Circle, Zone and head-office level.
Export of any report to PDF and spreadsheet formats, and the ability for an authorised officer to define a new report format by configuration.
4.6.3 Integration
The system shall support integration through documented, versioned and secured open APIs with the existing Integrated Command and Control Centre platform of the Corporation, with the citizen mobile application for grievance linkage and closure, and with the enforcement and challan-management system of the Corporation for issue of spot fines under the applicable Municipal Solid Waste bye-laws. The Bidder shall publish API documentation and provide a sandbox environment for integration testing. The Bidder is responsible for the platform side of each integration and for consuming the interface published by the counterpart system; it is not responsible for building or altering the counterpart system.
4.7 Civil, Electrical and Allied Works
4.7.1 Smart Pole and mounting infrastructure
All cameras, IoT and Public Address units, signage and networking equipment shall, so far as practicable, be mounted on a single integrated Smart Pole in preference to separate poles, so as to minimise clutter on public roads and footpaths and to present a uniform appearance across the city. The Smart Pole shall comprise:
A galvanised mild-steel or glass-reinforced-plastic pole of appropriate height, with weatherproof junction and termination box, cable routing conduit and tamper-proof access panel.
An integrated solar panel with charge controller and lithium battery bank, sized to power the camera, the IoT and Public Address unit and the signage independently at locations without reliable grid connectivity, with automatic changeover between grid and solar where grid power is available.
Provision for mounting the camera, the speaker unit, the warning LED or signage and the networking equipment on a single structure.
Earthing, lightning and surge protection, and reflective high-visibility base marking for road safety.
Where an existing pole or structure of the Corporation is available at a location and is assessed as suitable in the joint site survey, the Competent Authority may direct that it be used in place of a new Smart Pole. In that event the Smart Pole item shall not be payable for that location, and the mounting accessories and junction box shall be payable at the unit rates in the Price Schedule.
4.7.2 General civil and electrical works
Supply and erection of Smart Poles and mounting structures, junction boxes, cabling, conduiting and earthing as required at each location.
Coordination of power connection and metering with the local electricity distribution utility, or supply and installation of the solar power unit where grid power is unavailable or not feasible.
Weatherproofing, lightning and surge protection, and safety compliance for all outdoor installations.
Obtaining, with the facilitation of the Authority, such statutory approvals and right-of-way permissions as are required for erection of poles, and execution of the works consequent to such approvals.
Restoration of any public infrastructure or surface disturbed during installation to its original condition, to the satisfaction of the DEE (SWM) of concerned circle.
Any other civil, electrical or allied work required for the successful installation, commissioning and functioning of the system at a location, as may be directed by the DEE (SWM) of concerned circle.
4.8 Power and Connectivity
Each installation shall be powered from mains supply with battery backup of not less than eight (8) hours, or by a solar power pack with battery bank at locations lacking reliable grid connectivity, as determined during the joint site survey.
Connectivity shall be by 4G/5G or LTE using an IoT SIM, or by Wi-Fi or fibre backhaul where available, with store-and-forward capability sufficient to retain not less than twenty-four hours of event data and to synchronise automatically on restoration of connectivity, without loss of data and without duplicate records.
The Bidder shall supply the IoT SIM and the SIM module, and shall bear the recurring data charges for the contract period. The cost thereof shall be included in the rates quoted.
Recurring electricity charges for grid-connected locations shall be borne by the Authority. The Bidder shall coordinate metering and shall be responsible for the correctness of the connected load declared.
4.9 Operation and Maintenance
The Bidder shall provide Comprehensive Operation and Maintenance covering preventive and breakdown maintenance of all cameras, sensors, Public Address units, Smart Poles, solar and power equipment, networking equipment, central infrastructure and the software and dashboard, together with the manpower deployed under Clause 4.10, for the contract period.
Comprehensive Operation and Maintenance includes the supply and replacement of any part, component or whole unit that fails, at no additional cost to the Authority, save as provided at Clause 11.4 in respect of theft and vandalism.
The Bidder shall carry out periodic preventive maintenance visits, comprising lens cleaning, sensor calibration, connector and cable checks, solar panel cleaning, battery health check and firmware update, at intervals of not more than thirty days (30) at each location, and shall record each visit in a site-wise maintenance register maintained in the dashboard.
The Bidder shall operate a dedicated toll-free number and helpdesk, manned during the Operation and Maintenance period, for logging of complaints by field staff of the Corporation and by the general public.
The Bidder shall maintain an asset register in the dashboard recording, for every installation, the Collection Point Code, the equipment serial numbers, the date of commissioning, the maintenance history and the relocation history.
4.10 Manpower for Shift-wise Monitoring
The Bidder shall, as part of the Operation and Maintenance scope, depute adequately trained manpower for round-the-clock live monitoring of the dashboard at the Integrated Command and Control Centre, on a shift basis, for the entire Operation and Maintenance period.
Deployment shall be in three (3) shifts of eight (8) hours each covering operations on a twenty-four hour, seven day basis, with not fewer than four (4) trained operators per shift, together with a Shift Supervisor or Team Lead in each shift for day-to-day coordination with the Sanitation and Solid Waste Management Wing.
Deployed manpower shall be responsible for real-time observation of alerts, prompt escalation of unresolved or critical incidents to the concerned Sanitation Inspector, Deputy Executive Engineer (SWM) or Superintending Engineer(SWM), maintenance of shift-wise incident registers and logs, and coordination with the field maintenance team of the Bidder in respect of equipment faults.
The Bidder shall maintain attendance and duty rosters of deployed manpower and make them available for inspection by officers of the Corporation as and when required.
The Bidder shall be the employer of all such manpower and shall comply with all applicable labour legislation, including payment of not less than the minimum wages notified by the Government of Telangana, provident fund and employees state insurance contributions, and shall indemnify the Authority against any claim in that behalf. No employer-employee relationship shall arise between the Authority and any person deployed by the Bidder.
Manpower shall be paid for at the rate quoted at Item 13 of the Price Schedule, per operator per month.
4.11 Training and Capacity Building
The Bidder shall conduct hands-on training for operators at the Integrated Command and Control Centre, enforcement staff and technical maintenance personnel of the Corporation, on system operation, dashboard usage, incident handling, evidence retrieval and basic troubleshooting.
Training shall be delivered at Zone level and at the head office, and shall be repeated annually and on the release of any major functional change.
Comprehensive user manuals, standard operating procedures and as-built documentation for each location shall be handed over to the Authority, in English and Telugu.

5. Hosting, Availability and Disaster Recovery
5.1 Hosting Model and Responsibility
Hosting of all software, applications and data, whether on premise at the Integrated Command and Control Centre of the Corporation or in a data centre or cloud environment, shall be the sole responsibility of the Bidder, at its own cost, for the entire duration of the contract including the Operation and Maintenance period. No separate hosting charge shall be payable save as quoted at Item 12 of the Price Schedule.
Where the Bidder proposes a cloud environment, it shall be a Cloud Service Provider empanelled by the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology, Government of India.
All data at rest, including recorded video, event footage, metadata and personal data, shall be located within the territory of India, in compliance with the data-localisation and security norms of the Government of India and the Government of Telangana.
The subscription or licence shall be taken in the name of the Corporation where the provider permits, failing which in the name of the Bidder with a written undertaking to transfer or assign it to the Corporation on demand or on expiry or termination of the contract.
The Authority reserves the right to require migration of the environment to the Telangana State Data Centre, or to a Government Community Cloud, on ninety (90) days' written notice. In that event the Bidder shall migrate the environment at a cost agreed under Clause 8.5, and no early termination charge, exit fee or minimum commitment charge shall be payable to the Bidder or to any service provider.
5.2 Availability and Redundancy
The central platform shall be deployed with redundancy at the application and database tiers, with no single point of failure, automatic failover, and rolling deployment such that a release does not require the service to be taken down. The availability commitment, its method of measurement and the consequences of failure to meet it are set out at Clause 11.3.
5.3 Backup and Disaster Recovery
A disaster recovery environment shall be maintained in a location within India separate from the primary environment, with automated replication of configuration, metadata and event footage.
Recovery Point Objective not exceeding thirty (30) minutes and Recovery Time Objective not exceeding eight (8) hours in respect of the platform, dashboard and event data.
A full backup daily and incremental backup every six hours, with retention in accordance with Clause 4.3.3, checksum-based integrity validation of every backup, and a documented restoration test once each quarter, the result of which shall be reported to the Authority.
Encryption of all backups at rest.
A disaster recovery drill conducted once every six months in the presence of the Authority, with a report submitted within fifteen days of each drill.
Continuous recorded video held on edge storage at a location is not required to be replicated to the disaster recovery environment. Event footage, being the material of evidentiary value, shall be so replicated.
5.4 Monitoring
The Bidder shall provide, and shall give the Authority read access to, monitoring covering device health at every location, camera and sensor uptime, network and bandwidth utilisation, storage capacity and growth, power and battery status at solar locations, intrusion and tamper alerts, and automated measurement of the service levels at Clause 11.3, from which the monthly service report shall be generated. Service level measurement shall be from the monitoring system, and the underlying measurement data shall be available to the Authority. Service levels shall not be computed from figures self-reported by the Bidder without an auditable source.

6. Security, Compliance and Data Protection
6.1 Security Controls
Encryption of data at rest using AES-256 or equivalent, and in transit using TLS 1.2 or later, including the video stream from each location.
Secure device onboarding, with each camera and IoT unit uniquely identified and authenticated, and default credentials changed on commissioning.
Strict role-based access control on the principle of least privilege, and multi-factor authentication for all administrative and privileged accounts.
Immutable audit logging of every action performed, capturing user identity, timestamp, action, and the value before and after change, retained for the contract period and two years thereafter.
Detection and alerting on unauthorised access attempts and anomalous usage patterns, API rate limiting, input validation and web application firewall protection.
Secure software development practices, including static and dynamic application security testing before each major release.
Quarterly vulnerability assessment, and closure of critical and high vulnerabilities within seven and fifteen days respectively.
The Bidder shall be liable for any data breach attributable to inadequate hosting or device security.
6.2 Compliance and Certification
The platform shall be subjected to a security audit by an auditor empanelled with CERT-In, at the cost of the Bidder, before Go-Live and thereafter once every twelve months and upon every major release. A clear audit certificate is a condition precedent to Go-Live under Clause 8.1.
All cameras and IoT devices shall be BIS, CE or FCC certified as applicable, and shall comply with the technical guidelines of the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology and of the Smart Cities Mission for surveillance and IoT device security.
Preference shall be given to equipment complying with the applicable public procurement orders of the Government of India relating to Make in India, in accordance with the orders in force on the last date of bid submission.
The solution shall comply with the Information Technology Act, 2000 as amended, the Greater Hyderabad Municipal Corporation Act, 1955, the Telangana Municipal Solid Waste Management Bye-laws, and the Solid Waste Management Rules, 2016.
The Bidder shall procure and maintain a valid SSL or TLS certificate for all published endpoints throughout the contract period, at its own cost.
The project shall be implemented in accordance with the Swachh Bharat Mission (Urban) 2.0 Operational Guidelines issued by the Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs, and all deliverables, milestones and documentation shall additionally be structured so as to satisfy the reporting, audit and utilisation-certificate requirements of the Mission.
6.3 Data Protection and Surveillance Safeguards
The system will capture video of public places and of identifiable persons, and will generate material used in enforcement proceedings. The following obligations apply and are additional to, and not in substitution for, the confidentiality obligations at Clause 8.6.
For the purposes of the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, the Authority is the Data Fiduciary and the Bidder is a Data Processor acting solely on the instructions of the Authority.
The Bidder shall process personal data, including video footage and images of identifiable persons, only to the extent necessary to perform its obligations under the contract, and for no other purpose. The Bidder shall not use such data for product development, model training, benchmarking, analytics for its own account, demonstration to any third party, or any commercial purpose whatsoever.
The Bidder shall not publish, share, transmit or make available any footage to any person other than an officer of the Authority duly authorised in that behalf. Any request for footage from any other person, including from any law enforcement agency, shall be referred to the Authority and shall not be acted upon by the Bidder.
Cameras shall not be positioned so as to survey the interior of private premises, and privacy masking shall be applied in accordance with Clause 4.3.1.
No facial recognition, biometric identification or matching of any person against any database shall be undertaken by the Bidder, and no such capability shall be deployed, unless expressly directed in writing by the Authority under Clause 8.5 and subject to such conditions as the Authority may impose.
The Bidder shall implement and maintain reasonable security safeguards to prevent personal data breach, and shall maintain a record of processing activities which shall be made available to the Authority on request.
The Bidder shall not engage a sub-processor, and shall not permit any third party to access personal data, without the prior written consent of the Authority. Where consent is given, the Bidder shall remain liable for the acts and omissions of the sub-processor.
The Bidder shall notify the Authority of any personal data breach, or of any reasonable suspicion of a breach, within twenty-four (24) hours of becoming aware of it, and shall provide all assistance required by the Authority in intimating the Data Protection Board of India and affected Data Principals.
No personal data and no footage shall be transferred outside India.
Evidence captured shall be stored and produced in a manner admissible for enforcement action under the applicable bye-laws, with an unbroken chain of custody, and the system shall record and preserve the integrity metadata of any footage marked for evidentiary use.
On expiry or termination of the contract, the Bidder shall return all data to the Authority in an open, machine-readable format and shall thereafter erase it from all systems within its control, including backups, and shall certify such erasure in writing. Erasure shall be deferred only to the extent, and for so long as, retention is required by law or by pending proceedings.

9.4 Selection Methodology and Award
The overall evaluation shall be on the QCBS basis in the ratio of 70 : 30, seventy per cent weightage being given to the technical evaluation in accordance with Clause 9.3 and thirty per cent to the financial evaluation.
The Total Evaluated Bid Price for each responsive financial bid shall be the "Total Evaluated Bid Price" computed at the foot of the Price Schedule at C Form #2, being the sum of the extended amounts of every item of that Schedule, computed by applying the unit rate quoted against each item to the evaluation quantity stated against that item.
The financial score of each Bidder shall be computed as: F = 100 × (lowest Total Evaluated Bid Price among all responsive bids ÷ Total Evaluated Bid Price of the Bidder). The Bidder quoting the lowest Total Evaluated Bid Price shall accordingly obtain a financial score of 100.
The overall score of each Bidder shall be computed as: S = (0.70 × Technical Score) + (0.30 × Financial Score), each score being out of 100.
The proposals shall be ranked in terms of the overall scores obtained, from highest to lowest. The Bidder obtaining the highest overall score shall be considered for award of the contract and shall be issued the Letter of Intent.
Where two or more Bidders obtain the same overall score, the Bidder with the higher technical score shall be preferred. Where the technical scores are also equal, the Bidder with the lower Total Evaluated Bid Price shall be preferred.
The Authority reserves the right to negotiate with the Bidder whose proposal has been ranked first, on the basis of best value to the Project. Negotiation shall not result in an increase in any quoted unit rate or in any dilution of the Scope of Work.
The evaluation quantities in the Price Schedule are used solely to arrive at a comparable Total Evaluated Bid Price. Payment shall be made on the quantities actually supplied, installed, relocated and maintained, at the unit rates quoted, in accordance with Clause 11.2.

Illustration. A Bidder obtaining a technical score of 78 marks, whose Total Evaluated Bid Price is Rs. 62.50 Crores against a lowest responsive price of Rs. 55.00 Crores, obtains a financial score of 100 × (55.00 ÷ 62.50) = 88.00, and an overall score of (0.70 × 78) + (0.30 × 88.00) = 54.60 + 26.40 = 81.00.
